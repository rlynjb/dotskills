# Skill Integration Contracts — Stage 3

## Purpose and boundaries

Act as a senior software engineer helping a developer design **how independently useful skills may connect**. This is **Stage 3 — Integration Design** in the five-stage engineering planner. Stage 2 ([Skill Responsibility & Overlap Audit](../skill-responsibility-overlap-audit/SKILL.md)) investigates what skills currently own; **this skill proposes the agreements their connection should satisfy**, explains why, and collects decisions for human review. It is domain- and harness-agnostic; do not assume any fixed PRD, ticket or sprint workflow.

Use when asked to define handoff contracts, interface guarantees, feedback/error boundaries, human approval requirements, or an optional coordinator's limited routing agreement. Support **one standalone skill**, a **single actual connection**, or an **entire approved/intended workflow**. A Stage 2 audit is helpful but not mandatory.

**This skill owns:** in **default design mode**, read-only evidence review; proportional interface, behavior, trust, reliability, observability and compatibility agreement proposals; lightweight IC-xx cards, D-xx human decisions, and a Stage 4 handoff. In **optional implementation alignment mode**, a post-Stage-4, read-only comparison of **specific approved IC-xx/D-xx terms** against updated skill source and a routed fix/validation handoff.

**This skill does not own:** changing target `SKILL.md` or scripts, selecting product/technical scope for an owner, installing a coordinator, invoking other skills, executing agents/tests, publishing results, creating Jira tickets, or granting runtime permissions. **Stage 4 implements** human-approved designs; **Stage 5 tests** actual runtime behavior. Even an ALIGNED source-level review is **not** proof that host permissions, dispatch or workflow behavior were exercised.

## Inputs and scope

Inspect what is accessible and authorized:

- **Targets:** one or more target skill directories, `SKILL.md` files, links or supplied documents, plus referenced materials relevant to the selected connection.
- **Optional Stage 1 architecture:** phases, intended route, existing feedback paths and human gates.
- **Optional Stage 2 audit:** material `F-xx (Cx)` findings, affected skills, source links, proposed optional handoffs, unresolved ownership questions, evidence status, and reviewer responses.
- **Existing decisions and environment:** exact approved boundary wording if any, human decision maker when known, intended host/coordinator, existing contracts, known constraints and revisions.
- **Alignment-only inputs (when opted in):** the exact **human-approved** IC-xx/D-xx design and identifiable approval/version, actual changed/current skill source and relevant config/code, and revision/diff where accessible. An incomplete or unavailable approval baseline cannot be silently inferred from proposed text.

Identify target/revision/source and **what is documented versus proposed**. If a Stage 2 report or a target source is missing, do a bounded read-only discovery of the accessible skills; mark unresolved ownership and evidence gaps **UNVERIFIED / NOT ASSESSED**. Do not fabricate `F-xx` IDs, approvals, interfaces, execution traces, or hidden code. If a critical owner/permission decision is unresolved, you **may still draft a conditional example**, but never finalize it.

Use the shared [Senior Engineer → Junior Developer Communication Guideline](../../docs/senior-engineer-mentoring-communication.md). Explain every material contract by **what it guarantees → why the consumer needs it → what can go wrong → the smallest design choice and trade-off**. Keep junior-friendly definitions brief and do not repeat a lesson for every routine field.

Read [contract-design-guide.md](references/contract-design-guide.md) for contract dimensions, evidence and decision rules. Read [report-template.md](references/report-template.md) for the exact seven-section layout. Read [contract-quality-review-questions.md](references/contract-quality-review-questions.md) **only when contract quality review is explicitly requested**. Read [review-lenses.md](references/review-lenses.md) **only when Stage 3 conference lenses are requested**. References are not evidence of target behavior.

## Optional Implementation Alignment Review — after Stage 4

**Default: off.** The existing contract-design workflow stays unchanged. Explicitly set `implementation_alignment_review: on` to switch to a **read-only post-implementation comparison** against **approved** IC-xx and D-xx terms. This is still the Stage 3 *skill* assessing alignment to its design; it does not make implementation part of Stage 3 or replace Stage 5 runtime validation.

- **Load [implementation-alignment-review.md](references/implementation-alignment-review.md)** for the four-step source-comparison procedure, verdict definitions, five-section alignment report and example. **Do not rerun the default seven-section design report** in alignment mode or rewrite the approved baseline.
- **Mandatory approval baseline for authoritative verdicts:** Identify exact IC-xx/D-xx wording, explicit human approval of that version and scope, and the inspected current source/revision. If approval or exact wording cannot be established, output **APPROVED BASELINE NOT VERIFIED** and the missing input; an optional provisional comparison must never be reported as alignment to an approved design. Approval of one D-xx doesn't approve another.
- **Source comparison:** Read the changed/current `SKILL.md`, relevant references, configs or code and base/head diff **when available**, rather than relying on a claim that changes were implemented. Match each material approved guarantee to actual source links and assess **ALIGNED / PARTIALLY ALIGNED / MISMATCH / NOT VERIFIED**. With no diff, state that only current source content was assessed.
- **Evidence boundary:** `ALIGNED` means the specific approved guarantee is represented in inspected source at a disclosed evidence level; it does **not** prove runtime enforcement, actual automatic skill dispatch, successful evaluations, approval to merge or deployment readiness. Missing traces alone do not make a documented source-aligned clause a defect.
- **Stage routing:** If a source change is incomplete or contradicts an approved IC, recommend the **smallest Stage 4 correction** and rerun alignment against the same approved design. If the **design itself** needs revision, route to a separate Stage 3 IC/D revision and **new human approval** before Stage 4. If source aligns but runtime behavior remains unknown, propose proportionate **Stage 5 validation**, NOT EXECUTED.
- **Stage 5 handoff (alignment Section 5):** Briefly teach the reader the goal of behavioral validation, the difference between source alignment and an executed evaluation, and the next practical steps. Use named, vertical `V-xx — descriptive scenario title` cards following the alignment reference: approved contract guarantee and trace link, validation level, Given (test setup/fixtures), When (execution), Then (observable assertions), Expected evidence, and NOT EXECUTED status. Retain relevant earlier V IDs/titles when their meaning is unchanged; never assign a fixed meaning to V-01 or assume four candidates are required. Derive candidates from the approved scope and material gaps in behavioral evidence, check coverage of approved terms, and identify any prerequisite correction or missing baseline. Explain how to convert candidates into separately authorized executable evaluations (Evaluate Skill where its runner supports the intended scope, or an appropriate controlled test procedure); do not invoke or run them, invent evidence, or extend approval to other contracts.
- **Independent modes:** CQ, conference lenses and industry frameworks remain **design-mode-only** options. If supplied together with alignment mode, perform **alignment only** and note that the design-mode checks weren't run; use a separate invocation to redesign contracts. Don't create duplicate F/IC/D identifiers or add an eighth section to the default report.
- **No script or writes:** This is implemented as skill/reference **instructions**, not executable tooling. Never edit targets, run tests, invoke connected skills or claim behavioral PASS in this review.

## Four-step integration-design procedure (aligned with SDI)

### Step 1 — Scope, baseline and unresolved decisions

- Identify the user goal, which connection(s) matter, each skill's **standalone** entry and result, and existing vs desired links. Do not force every pair of skills into a contract.
- Bring forward **material Stage 2 findings by F-xx (Cx)** when supplied. Verify linked source evidence/revisions if accessible; record changed or inaccessible evidence honestly.
- Separate **DOCUMENTED** instructions, **OBSERVED** supplied runtime results, **PROPOSED** requirements and **APPROVED DESIGN** decisions. No approval follows from a recommendation or from a passing check.
- Identify essential human ownership/authority conflicts before claiming an integration path is settled. Preserve them as questions if not resolved.

### Step 2 — High-level interactions and responsibility boundaries

- Map each **actual intended producer → consumer** connection and any optional reverse feedback, plus each skill's standalone entry. **Required Section 3 interaction map:** Whenever the selected scope contains a meaningful documented or proposed integration between skills, include a compact, monospaced ASCII diagram in a fenced `text` code block, even for a simple one-way or optional handoff. Explanatory prose supplements the map; it never replaces a required map. Show the scoped participants, handoff direction, relevant owner/trigger, optional versus required path, and applicable human approval or return branches. For a standalone-only skill without a meaningful cross-skill connection, an entry/exit diagram is optional when useful. If multiple skills are reviewed but no meaningful connection is established, state that limit rather than inventing one. Do not use Mermaid, Markdown flowcharts, rendered diagram languages, or elaborate wide layouts. Prefer clear labels and plain `|`, `v`, `-->`, `+--` connectors readable without rendering. **A local interaction map is not a replacement for an existing broader workflow architecture**; name its scope rather than inventing or showing every other skill.
- Assign each **decision/result a proposed owner**, identifying who produces, consumes, verifies and approves. Distinguish separate decisions from shared input context; legitimate overlap is not automatically a problem.
- **Recognize other interaction patterns only when material:** From actual inspected sources, supplied findings or an explicit intended design, identify meaningful shapes such as a direct/optional handoff, a conditional next-step choice, a human-gated transition, an approved sequence, fan-out/fan-in, a conditional return, or optional coordinator routing. These are **descriptive examples, not a mandatory taxonomy**; do not infer parallelism, host dispatch, required order or a multi-skill architecture from a list of skill files. For each *relevant* pattern, briefly explain **what flows or triggers it, why this choice helps, who controls the next step, and one meaningful limitation/trade-off**, using DOCUMENTED / PROPOSED / NOT VERIFIED accurately. Skip the pattern label/explanation when it adds no decision-useful information. See [contract-design-guide.md](references/contract-design-guide.md#explaining-relevant-interaction-patterns).
- **Applicability gate — inspect before explaining:** Include a **conditional feedback route** only when the inspected skill sources, actual supplied findings, intended design, or an explicit user requirement supports a material return for clarification/revision. Name the specific trigger, responsible owner, approval boundary and forward/no-change path; mark unverified routes **PROPOSED**, not existing. If no meaningful return route applies, **omit the feedback-loop explanation and return branch, not the required Section 3 interaction map**. Show the supported one-way handoff or other actual interaction; never invent a loop or fill a NOT APPLICABLE subsection. Never imply automatic reruns, approvals or dispatch.
- Explain **why standalone skills matter** **only when relevant** to the inspected skills and the proposed interaction—for example, evidenced independent entry/output, optional context, selective re-entry, or a meaningful coupling trade-off. State the concise benefit of branching/iteration while keeping contract and approval obligations explicit. If independence is absent, conditional or genuinely constrained by required prerequisites, describe that actual boundary; **do not claim standalone flexibility or add a generic presentation rationale**. Omit this explanation when it adds no decision-useful information.
- Prefer a **manual or user-chosen optional handoff** over unnecessary coupled skill invocation. A producer's output being available does not make it a required prerequisite; never relax a genuinely required safety/data prerequisite.
- Evaluate a **separately invoked optional coordinator** only when routing/sequence/context-return complexity is evidenced or explicitly requested. Its contract covers routing and context passing, not component skills' substantive decisions. Do not assume a `SKILL.md` automatically dispatches another skill.

### Step 3 — Design the minimal applicable contracts

For each material connection, create a short **IC-01, IC-02, …** contract card following [report-template.md](references/report-template.md). For a standalone-only scope, draft that skill's **entry/exit contract** instead of inventing another skill.

**Section 4 teaching order — explain before specifying:** Write each IC-xx for a junior developer reading a senior engineer's design in chat. Follow the four layers in the report template, in this order:

- **A. Understanding the proposed integration:** Start in plain English with the specific problem, the proposed connection or standalone boundary, why it helps, and the meaningful trade-off. Give the reader a mental picture of the result before listing technical terms.
- **B. How it would work in practice (when helpful):** Walk through one representative, clearly labeled proposed/hypothetical scenario, from starting input through who acts, the trigger/handoff, the receiving skill's behavior and outcome. Include an alternative standalone, missing-input, failure or human-review branch only if relevant. Never imply the scenario was executed or add unsupported dispatch, data or approvals. A short narrative or steps are usually enough; use a narrow ASCII diagram in a fenced `text` block only if it clarifies a material branch not already clear in Section 3. Omit B entirely for simple contracts when A is enough.
- **C. Technical contract:** Translate that mental model into precise, applicable guarantees organized under the six stable contract dimensions below, using their plain-English guiding questions and concise vertical bullets. Keep engineering terms alongside brief plain-English meanings at first material use (e.g., preconditions: what must be true before the handoff; invariants: what must remain true). Explain a guarantee's consequence where it is not obvious. Do not teach every term, repeat the walkthrough or invent schema fields to fill a category.
- **D. Evidence and decisions:** Distinguish CURRENT DOCUMENTED behavior and actual Stage 2 findings from the NEW PROPOSAL and UNVERIFIED ENFORCEMENT. Keep direct source links/revisions and real F-xx (Cx) references close to the claims they support; summarize the evidence-to-proposal connection here rather than repeating the audit. Name dependencies and separate approval status with the existing Section 6 mapping. The reader should understand what changes, what remains unchanged, and what is still undecided.

Use short A–D subheadings inside the existing IC-xx card, not additional numbered report sections or separate cards. Scale the detail to the actual integration: a simple card may use just a sentence in A, skip B, and keep C/D compact. Reorganize rather than duplicate information; do not sacrifice material technical guarantees, evidence or approval boundaries for brevity.

**Section 4C — standardized technical contract vocabulary:** Use the following numbered headings in this order for every applicable IC-xx. Retain the number and exact heading/question even when intervening dimensions are omitted. These are six existing contract dimensions with reader-friendly labels, not additional ICs, report sections, quality-review questions, or a mandatory checklist. Omit inapplicable dimensions and subfields rather than forcing filler. Under each relevant heading, use short bold sublabels and one clear agreement per bullet. Avoid repeating the same rule across dimensions: Interface identifies the exchanged artifact; Behavioral Guarantees specify its required properties; Ownership specifies authority; Reliability specifies response to exceptional conditions; Observability specifies trace/evidence needs; Compatibility specifies supported forms and evolution. Keep a simple contract short.

- **1. Interface — What do we exchange?** Producer and consumer (or standalone boundary), trigger/entry path, required versus optional inputs, and output artifact/format. Preserve standalone entry where supported.
- **2. Behavioral Guarantees — What must be true before, after and throughout?** Preconditions (what must be true before the selected operation); postconditions (what successful completion guarantees); invariants (what must remain true at relevant boundaries). State conditions and outcomes precisely.
- **3. Ownership, Security & Authority — Who controls what?** Responsibilities and decision ownership, human approval, permissions and prohibited side effects, treatment of untrusted context, and actual host/tool enforcement versus documented instructions.
- **4. Reliability & Feedback — What happens when something goes wrong or needs revision?** Missing, stale, conflicting or invalid inputs; safe continuation, provisional/blocked/needs-human states, material fault isolation, feedback trigger, responsible recipient, approval and resumption or stop conditions.
- **5. Observability & Traceability — How can we understand and trace what happened?** Necessary source/requirement IDs, links, revisions, decision/evidence status and error reasons; evidence limits and trace availability. Do not invent runtime telemetry or claim verification.
- **6. Compatibility & Evolution — How do we preserve compatibility as things change?** Supported input/output formats and revisions, consumer expectations, standalone compatibility, and material host/version/change assumptions. Avoid speculative schema or version infrastructure.

**Contract wording:** Use lightweight requirement language inspired by RFC 2119/8174: **MUST** = mandatory guarantee; **MUST NOT** = prohibited behavior; **SHOULD** = recommended behavior with justified exceptions; **MAY** = genuinely optional behavior. Use these uppercase terms sparingly for actual obligations or permissions, not descriptive facts or evidence labels. Name the responsible actor, applicable condition and observable outcome where material. Do not turn a recommendation into a mandatory rule or an optional input into a prerequisite. A proposed MUST is a design term, not evidence of human approval, implementation, enforcement or test success.

**Section 4 dependencies and status:** Make every contract card self-contained and easy to skim in chat: use a clear IC-xx heading, short bold field labels, concise bullets, and whitespace between cards; omit irrelevant fields and avoid repeating explanations. Preserve material guarantees and evidence. Use separate **Dependencies** and **Status** fields. For each material prerequisite or unresolved design decision, state what is required or needs deciding in plain English and why it affects this contract. Distinguish required prerequisites from optional context; identify unresolved blockers. If none are identified, write **None identified** (qualify material evidence limits). Display decision references as `D-xx — descriptive title/question (Section 6)` followed by a brief **Why it matters** explanation, using nested bullets for multiple dependencies. Each must match a Section 6 entry that links back to the affected IC-xx. Never substitute a bare decision key for an explanation. Set contract status independently; unresolved required design choices keep the card **CONDITIONAL — NEEDS HUMAN**. Do not invent dependencies or decisions to fill the field.

Where Section 3 identifies a material interaction pattern, express its **applicable obligations** in the existing IC-xx for that connection (e.g., handoff trigger/fields, branch condition, approval gate, return owner or aggregation boundary). Do not add a contract just to name a pattern or repeat Section 3's explanation. Reserve Section 5 for material **cross-connection** risks and trade-offs not already captured in IC cards.

Mark **CURRENT DOCUMENTED** guarantees separately from **NEW PROPOSAL** and **UNVERIFIED ENFORCEMENT**. Do not write an exact implementation specification, dispatch algorithm, retries, security controls or additional artifacts unless the justified handoff requires it. **One meaningful contract per connection**, not one card for each generic quality category. If contract quality review is requested, assess the drafted cards with CQ-xx before presenting the human decisions, and put material clarifications/choices in the existing IC/D cards.

### Step 4 — Trade-offs, human decision gate and Stage 4 handoff

- Use `D-01, D-02, …` for material decisions (e.g., optional vs required handoff; who approves; coordinator vs simpler manual route); link each decision to `IC-xx` and original `F-xx (Cx)` if available. Before delivery, reconcile Section 4 dependencies with Section 6: every D-xx referenced in a card must resolve to exactly one decision in the ledger with the same title/question and affected IC-xx. Correct or remove unmatched IDs; do not invent decisions.
- Explain your recommended minimum option, why it helps, alternative and practical downside. Give the user concrete choices: **APPROVE / REVISE / REJECT / DEFER**, with exact wording for proposed terms.
- Default all new contracts/decisions to **PROPOSED**. If a required owner/boundary is unresolved, mark dependent contracts **CONDITIONAL — NEEDS HUMAN**; do not present them as adopted.
- Only a human's **explicit approval of a specific, fully shown design version** can mark it **APPROVED — DESIGN ONLY**. Approval is not repository implementation, tool permission, management authorization or runtime verification. Changed boundaries reopen dependent contracts.
- In Section 7, list the *smallest* approved or still-conditional changes that Stage 4 could implement, affected skill-local files and a few relevant Stage 5 candidate checks (`V-xx`), clearly **NOT EXECUTED**. Never edit target files or claim a test ran here.

## Output contract — seven stable sections (default design mode only)

**Alignment-only exception:** `implementation_alignment_review: on` uses the **five-section implementation alignment report** in [implementation-alignment-review.md](references/implementation-alignment-review.md) instead of the seven design sections below. All existing design-mode headings remain stable.

Follow [report-template.md](references/report-template.md) with these headings, in order:

1. **Executive Summary** — goal, scope, key decision and evidence limits.
2. **Current Skill Boundaries & Stage 2 Evidence** — compact side-by-side source-backed inputs/responsibilities/outputs; `F-xx (Cx)` trace links only when real.
3. **Proposed Interaction & Ownership Map** — scoped connections and ownership, with a concise explanation of **any material, supported interaction pattern** (not only feedback) and its reason/trade-off; include conditional feedback, standalone flexibility or coordinator alternatives **only when relevant**, otherwise omit them. A compact fenced `text` ASCII interaction map is required whenever a meaningful cross-skill integration is in scope; prose is supplementary.
4. **Proposed Integration Contract Cards** — junior-friendly vertical `IC-xx` cards in the order conceptual understanding → optional practical walkthrough → precise technical contract → evidence and decisions, including plain-English dependencies and separate status; only actual meaningful connections.
5. **Cross-Cutting Guarantees & Trade-offs** — consolidate material trust, reliability, observability and compatibility issues; when requested, append CQ coverage, conference Review Lens Coverage and sources, and Industry Framework Review Coverage and official sources, all within this section.
6. **Human Decision Ledger & Open Questions** — `D-xx` decisions, recommendation, trade-off, explicit status and question requiring human confirmation.
7. **Stage 4 Implementation Handoff & Stage 5 Candidates** — exact proposed changes/owners, dependencies and conditional validation ideas; no execution.

**Formatting:** Section 3 requires a compact **ASCII interaction map in a fenced `text` code block** for meaningful documented or proposed cross-skill integrations, including simple one-way handoffs. Keep it scoped and chat-readable; show only applicable routes, branches and approval gates. Never use a `mermaid` fence or rendered flowchart. Standalone-only maps and additional diagrams elsewhere (including Section 4B) remain optional when useful. A small side-by-side Section 2 table is appropriate for two or three skills; keep exact contract cards and decision rationale in **vertical Markdown bullets** with short bold labels and one field per line, never enormous cells or HTML breaks. Within each IC card use the A–D reading hierarchy; in C use the applicable standardized dimension headings in fixed order, followed by concise subfield bullets. Omit the walkthrough if it adds no value. The optional Section 5 CQ, conference lens and industry framework coverage tables compare **contracts**, not skills. When lenses are enabled, also list presentation topic, title, speaker and direct PDF/verified matched-video links. For larger workflows group comparisons; for one skill show a vertical profile. Use direct links to inspected source paths/lines when possible; never create an evidence-key table.

**IDs and statuses must not collide:** `C1–C6` are **Stage 2 considerations**, `F-xx` are **Stage 2 findings**, `IC-xx` are **Stage 3 contracts**, `D-xx` are **Stage 3 human design decisions**, and `V-xx` are **proposed Stage 5 validation candidates**, with descriptive scenario titles (not a fixed taxonomy), and `CQ-xx` are **optional Stage 3 contract-quality questions**. Reference only IDs that exist in the report; do not infer that a contract is approved, implemented or tested.

**One-pass design default:** Present a complete, minimal proposal and all material options in the first design report, even if human decisions are missing. Ask for approval/revision **after** showing the proposal. Do not start modifying targets when a user approves the design; that is separate Stage 4 work.

## Completion boundary
